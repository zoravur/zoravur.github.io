This post describes the process that I took to go on a backpacking trip in Europe during this past April.

**Note: Deciding you want to travel**
- I was indecisive about whether I wanted to travel for longer than I care to admit. But in case you're feeling overwhelmed by the choice of whether 
and where to travel: know that you won't know what it's like until you do it. Just pick the destination that fascinates you the most and figure out 
why by going there. The world is vast. You won't see everything. You won't even see everything in the city you live in. So just go, and know that the world is 
inexhaustible, but you are not insatiable.

**Before the trip (2 months)**
- Book your flight early. In general, the earlier you go back, the more likely you are to save, though this only works up to a point. 
- Google Flights is a good search engine for flight pricing, and it seemed a standout choice to find flights among everyone who I met during my travels.
    - Pro tip: enter a starting location and hit explore to see different locations you could travel to and their prices. It's cheaper if you do one way, 
        which is also preferred for backpacking, where your schedule might change spontaneously.
- In addition, book 2 weeks of accommodation prior to departure.
- HostelWorld is great for booking hostels. You can generally trust their reviews.

**Packing List**
- Outlet adapter for where you're going. [This one I bought](https://www.amazon.ca/dp/B09XCHHWRR) is supposed to cover the entire world, but I just used the EU connector.
- Earplugs (people snore!!)
- Eye mask
- Flip-flops for hostel showers
- Melatonin / any medications
- A lock for hostel lockers. Any padlock will do.
- Laundry pods -- though fair warning, you may not have access to machines and might have to go on a side quest. Regardless, it's good to be prepared.
- Clothing
